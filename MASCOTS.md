# Mascot brainstorm

The mascot is the park's announcer: on the entrance sign, introducing each game, handing out prizes. It has to read as "1950s carnival", work as a simple low-poly model, and be recognisable as a flat silhouette on a ticket.

All of these are original characters.

## The candidates

### 1. Colonel Kernel — a popcorn box in a ringmaster's coat
- **Look:** red-and-white striped popcorn carton for a body, popcorn puffing out the top like a pompadour, tiny tailcoat, waxed moustache, cane.
- **Personality:** pompous showman who oversells everything. "The MOST thrilling snail race on EARTH!"
- **Why it works:** the simplest shape to model (a box), instantly reads as carnival, and popcorn can burst out of his head when someone wins.
- **Risk:** food mascots can feel generic.

### 2. Dizzy the Carousel Horse — escaped from the merry-go-round
- **Look:** chipped painted carousel horse, brass pole still through her back, one eye slightly off, faded roses on the saddle.
- **Personality:** sweet, a bit dazed from decades of spinning, wanders in circles when idle.
- **Why it works:** the "faded paint" theme is built into the character; the pole makes a great silhouette; she can lead the crowd around the park map.
- **Risk:** four legs is the hardest thing here to animate.

### 3. Mr. Fizzwick — a firework who never went off
- **Look:** a stubby rocket with a paper wrapper, a face on the cone, and a fuse on his head that is always *slightly* lit.
- **Personality:** anxious optimist. Every finale he thinks "tonight's the night". At the end of the night he finally launches and becomes the fireworks.
- **Why it works:** gives the whole evening a story with a payoff; the fuse doubles as a timer in every game; ties directly into Hot Potato Bomb.
- **Risk:** needs care so he doesn't read as a bomb.

### 4. Tick-It — a living admission ticket
- **Look:** a stubby cream ticket with perforated edges, "ADMIT ONE" across his belly, stick arms and legs, bow tie.
- **Personality:** eager junior employee on his first day; takes the rules very seriously.
- **Why it works:** flat, so trivial to build and animate; matches the tickets on players' phones and the prize-ticket scoring, so the whole pack shares one visual idea.
- **Risk:** the least "1950s" of the set unless the typography carries it.

### 5. Madame Bulb — the marquee light who runs the place
- **Look:** a big warm-glow lightbulb with eyelashes, a brass screw-base skirt and a feather boa of string lights.
- **Personality:** glamorous old-Hollywood diva; calls everyone "darling"; flickers when annoyed.
- **Why it works:** she *is* the park's lighting, so she can appear anywhere a sign is; glowing looks great at dusk in 3D.
- **Risk:** glow effects cost performance on slow computers.

### 6. Sprocket the Strongman Bear — a wind-up tin toy
- **Look:** tin bear in a striped one-piece, handlebar moustache, big wind-up key in his back, visible seams and rivets.
- **Personality:** gentle giant who winds down mid-sentence and needs a crank.
- **Why it works:** tin-toy construction suits low-poly; winding down is a natural "waiting for players" animation; he *is* the giant stuffed animal's cousin for the finale.
- **Risk:** bears are the most common mascot animal.

### 7. Gus the Goldfish — the prize nobody won
- **Look:** a goldfish in a round bowl balanced on a tiny unicycle (or carried by a balloon), wearing a boater hat.
- **Personality:** dry, sarcastic commentator who has watched people lose at ring toss since 1954.
- **Why it works:** a sarcastic announcer suits adult party humour; a bowl is a sphere, so very cheap to model.
- **Risk:** limited body language; he mostly has a face.

## Recommendation

**Mr. Fizzwick as the mascot, with Tick-It as the phone-side helper.**

- Fizzwick is the only candidate who gives the night a beginning, middle and end: he's nervous at the gate, his fuse burns down as tickets pile up, and when the host closes the park he finally goes off as the fireworks over the prize ceremony.
- His fuse is a ready-made timer for every game.
- Tick-It lives on the phones (the player's ticket, waiting screens, "you earned 80 tickets!"), which keeps the TV character and the phone character distinct.

Runner-up: **Colonel Kernel**, if a louder, jokier announcer is preferred. He's also the cheapest to build.
